The Recipes

Things on Biscuits

Many people did not recognise the things on biscuits. Some people where worried about cholesterol, allergies and just being sick, but they really are very Spanish and very safe if you wash your hands before you make them. We were not trying to bring back memories of the shits and encourage more trips to the Torca honest.

Ingredients:

Packets of cheap dry biscuits (avoid Ryvita it's too English to create a Spanish effect)
Tins of mussels, razor fish, prawns in strange sauce etc.
Tins of Tuna (Spanish)
Ketchup (not Heinz, anything cheap and not recognisable)

Method:

Arrange biscuits on red and yellow plastic plates (Gives Spanish feel, colours of flag). Open tins and put contents on top. Mix Tuna with cheap tomato sauce put on rest of biscuits with a plastic spoon and squash it a bit. Put rest of cheap tomato sauce in little blobs on the other things for decoration.

Olives

Ingredients:

Olives from a tin
Bits of dried red pepper and pickled onions.
Grass and herbs.

Method:

Open tin, empty olives and other ingredients into another plastic bowl. (We borrowed ours from a neighbour, which made a change from the red and yellow plastic plates)

Interestingly Spread Cheese

This one was very popular with the Brits. (Maybe because they recognised it)

Ingredients:

Tubs of long life cheese spread of different varieties (not Philadelphia, too expensive)
1 tin black Olives
Same yellow or red plastic plates
Round dry biscuits (to ring the changes from square ones)

Method:

I suppose it's getting obvious by now, just make sure that when you stick the black olive in the middle, it is exactly in the centre or they will look rather lopsided and spoil the effect.

And now for the favourite and most mystical recipe of all time which is really Helen's:

Waldorf Salad a la Tresviso 2000

There is an interesting story behind this rather complicated but very worthwhile recipe which is worth telling.

As we wandered round the hypermarket the word Waldorf kept cropping up in Helen's imagination and she knew it had something to do with celery. We had just bought some at the vegetable stall. As we wandered round the dried and tinned food department a flash of inspiration flashed up and Helen remembered the name of the old friend who had once served up this delicacy. Without wasting a moment Helen whipped out her modern mobile phone which had coverage in the depths of a Spanish hypermarket and quickly discovered the main ingredients, which the hypermarket did not stock except for the Walnuts. (Hence the name). So we improvised and of course instead of Stilton, we left the cheese for last, knowing the local produce of the place we were heading. The lettuces were imported to Tresviso carefully packed in ice. If you take a bag of ice to your local supermarket to buy the lettuce, we can almost guarantee the same results, otherwise see our website in the future and we will do our best to serve local lettuces packed in Spanish Ice.

Ingredients:

Bag of Walnuts

Lettuce on ice
Celery

Tresviso Cheese

Other things we had fresh on ice

Method:

Get Tony Coyte to spend an afternoon shelling the Walnuts. Keep an eye on how many he eats or buy double quantity. Empty dog biscuits out of Tupperware container and send Tony Coyte to wash it. Meanwhile remove ice from fresh ingredients and chop up fresh ingredients. Open a bottle of wine and after a few glasses carry on chopping up the fresh ingredients. Rescue the fresh ingredients from the grass. Open another bottle of wine. Pick out the grass from the fresh ingredients and put them in the now dry Tupperware container. Add nuts and cheese and stir. Not too vigorously or it all ends up back in the grass again. Salad dressing: forget about Tupperware container a bit and leave it out in the rain while you open another bottle of wine. Retrieve from rain about ten minutes before guests arrive.
