  1. Introduction

    2. After a decades absence the Lancaster University Speleological Society returned to the Eastern Massif of the Picos de Europa in August 1994, to continue the expeditions that twenty years earlier led to the discovery of the Cueva del Agua resurgence and the renowned systems of Tere, Sara, and Sima 56.

    Long on motivation but short on gear, experience or manpower, we knew it was an impressive mantel we were attempting to inherit. This was evident from the deeds of our LUSS predecessors recorded in the club's journals. These journals contained heady portraits of massive expeditions with tons of gear, Land Rovers and dozens of team members, some of whom are today amongst the most well known cavers in the sport.

    Our daunting goal was to accomplish that which the legendary expeditions in days of yore could not find: a new entrance to Cueva Del Agua, at an altitude mid way between it and the notorious Sima 56, thus allowing possible surveying of Agua's upper level fossil passages without the time and gear consuming descent/ascent required by the two known entrances. Although such a 56 to Agua connection with a total depth of greater than 1700 metres is no longer in the running for the worlds deepest system (as it was in the late '70s), it will still result in one of the deepest through trips in the world.

    The 1994 expedition focused on a cave initially discovered by Colin Boothroyd and John Wallwork, the latter of which is now employed at the Hotel Torrecredos in Arenas de Cabralas by a former South Wales Caving Club member named Jim Thompson. Both Jim and John provided unexpected and substantial assistance during Tresviso 94, and proved invaluable in-country liasons for the team.

    Once used for cheese storage, the focal point of 94 came too be referred to as Cueva del Cuesos, and is located ???, roughly ??? metres from the highest known passages of Agua, and some 3 (horizontal) km from the terminal sump of Sima 56. However, after a short but sporting entrance and six successive pitches (the third and largest being 33 metres) the exploration halted at a draughting constriction 93 metres down. At the very end of the expedition some ??? days of digging revealed another 5-10 metres of crawlway before the rift squeezed almost shut again. This second constriction occurs before a small cupboard sized chamber with a slit in the floor, thrugh which tossed rocks fall for three quarters of a second before bouncing further.

    The breese was still evident and it was time to de-rig, but the tantalising posibility of having found a passable streamway just beyond this key-hole motivated the return in July 1995.
