SLUGS REPORTS

A slither up Great Whernside

23 April 2022 · Published 2022-04-28

← Events · Blog Archive · Original blog post ↗

 


The Team, Richard behind camera

That greatest of Whernsides required a crack leadership team so that the victorious Slugs could claim to it’s summit. Obviously Ken and Anne were the only team even willing to contemplate this audacious exploit. So they duly put a date in the diary and informed the Slug hoi polloi.

…… then they decided to go elsewhere leaving the hapless likes of Jeanne Richard and Mike to pull off this daring stunt. Indeed it is not well remembered but entirely possible that the idea of taking a shortcut over Buckden Pike to get back to Kettlewell was Ken’s. Who knows perhaps it was Jeanne's. Anyway in his usual annoying way Mike bombarded people with alternative routes in alternative digital formats, so they would spend so long tinkering on their phones and laptops that they would have no time to pack their boots.

On the way to Hag Dike

When will the author ever get to the point? It’s OK I am nearly there. So the self selected group of slitherers comprised Jeanne, Richard, Colin Jackson, Simon Dawson and Mike Avison. Contingency planning and emergency support logistics were supplied by the Marvellous Magical Murphical. The plan was to walk up from Kettlewell to Providence Pot (some doubt about finding it but it turned out that Providence entrance could not be easier to find being in the middle of the footpath and the middle of the valley.) 

Providence Pot

And then up the Great Whernside via Hag Dyke bunk barn. Finally back to Kettlewell by one of 3 routes. 

When Hag Dyke hove into view the awesome steepness and awful cloud clag on the summit did cause a bit of murmuring dissent, but this was thoroughly stamped out and the summit cloud cleared as we approached. That didn’t stop the bitingly cold wind though. We sheltered behind a rock on the summit for a very quick snack lunch and headed off along the ridge before sidling down the side to reach the road at Tor Dike at the head of Coverdale. There was some discussion whether anything about Tor Dike was man made, since it is written in the script reserved for monuments. Needless to say none of us knew. 



Anyway at the road we turned towards Kettlewell and the plan was to divert on to footpaths twice on our way back partly so as to visit the splendid entrance of Dow Cave. In the end, though, due to time pressure and tiredness we just walked all the way on the very quiet road. Thereby reserving enough time to enjoy coffee and cake at the &then cafe. (Yes it really is called &then). 

&then

All in all it was a pleasant 7 mile hike, with good company and that bitingly cold wind.
