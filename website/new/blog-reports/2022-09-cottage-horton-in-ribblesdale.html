 Riverside Cottage: Horton in Ribblesdale. 

September 9th-11th 2022

On Friday night, Anne, Ken, Jeanne, Richard, Colin B, and Rob Jones arrived in Horton. The evening was split between the cottage and the Crown Inn, with beer and chat flowing in both. Rob (LUSS '73-'76) was on his first SLUGS meet, Colin serendipitously in the UK for a trade fair.

On Saturday Jeanne and Richard were joined by Mike and Murphy for a very pleasant walk, first along the Pennine Bridleway to Sulber Nick and then back to Horton on the Pennine Way. Back at the cottage in time to enjoy the late afternoon sun.

Meanwhile the rest of the party went to visit Illusion Pot. At first it looked like it might be aptly named or should be renamed Illusive Pot as Ken misjudged how far we had to walk and Colin disappeared over the hillside enjoying the nostalgic feeling of hunting for an entrance on acres of featureless moorland... but eventually the entrance was located and we lifted the lid.

Inside are 26m of iron ladders, vertically down into the cave below. Next a short section of typical Dales caving led us to the infamous sump/duck. Luckily for us with all the dry weather it did not need bailing and the water was reasonably low.

The sump/duck. Photo: Craven Pothole Club.

Illusion Pot is a very attractive cave, but it should be admitted at this point that when we got to the duck I discovered that my camera battery was discharged, so I was unable to take any photos. I will use some from my previous visit, taken by members of the Craven Pothole Club.

After the sump we were in Dale Barn 3 and a short stretch of caving led into the massive Expressway.

Expressway, Dale Barn 3. Photo Brendan Marris.

Stomping along through this we soon came to an area of fantastic and unusual formations. Here we had to retrace our steps, and we now explored the passage in the other direction.

The passage continues as a large phreatic tube partly filled with glacial material, with many more impressive formations.

Expressway, photo from CPC trip. Photo CPC.

Eventually it closes down and a crawl  pops out in  Rushton  Hall, a large chamber roofed with many fine straws and semi-transparent curtains hanging down.

Brunel rift. Photo Craven Pothole Club.

Down on the left is the deep and intimidating Brunel Rift, where a cow's tail on the entertaining traverse is essential for safety. Shortly after this we arrived at a very inviting sump. Beyond this sump is Dale Barn 2 and after one more sump, Dale Barn 1. This emerges in Chapel le Dale, so the cave traverses under Scales Moor from Kingsdale to Chapel le Dale. However, it was time for us to return to the surface.

Another fine passage. Photo Brendan Marris.
Back at Horton, we were joined by Kevin and John who had been mountain biking around Ingleborough. Also Baxter joined us for a cup of tea. We all got changed and showered and then enjoyed an excellent meal together in the Crown Inn. Rob Jones met up with  Kevin after an interval of 45 years! The pub got noisier and noisier as more and more walkers arrived back after completing the Three Peaks, so once again we adjourned to the cottage for a more relaxing atmosphere.

Colin enjoying a beer.

Anne and Kevin enjoying the chat

On Sunday Kevin, Colin, Rob Jones and two members of the CPC had a nice trip down Committee Pot into Notts II. Ken and John had to miss out after they were involved in a collision with a motorcyclist, who tried to overtake John's van on a bend, collided with a Range Rover and then bounced back to collide with John's van. Luckily he only broke his ankle but his motorbike and the Range Rover were written off and John and his van had to be towed back to Keighley for repairs. The police gave Ken a lift back to the cottage. Meanwhile oblivious to all the drama on the roads Anne ran over Ingleborough and then Penyghent in the warmth of the autumn sunshine.

Summit of Penyghent.

A busy weekend; two fine caving trips, a walk and a run plus a mountain bike ride. Ten Slugs took part at various times.
