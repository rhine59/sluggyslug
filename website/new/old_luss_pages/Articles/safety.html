Safety Code

The following safety code will not include all possible suggestions on how to cave safely. Indeed opinions on how to cave safely have changed over the years and now may be contradictory. There is no substitute for experience in a cave no matter how many caving manuals you have read.

Remember, if you follow the following guidelines then will enjoy your trip and live to tell the adventures you have.

Before Leaving Lancaster

So you're about to go caving.
  • Make sure you go with at least one other person. A group of four is probably best. Caving solo is not recomended.
  • Choose a cave that is within the limits of the group. You can find out about caves both by talking to other people(best) and by looking in various caving books/magazines(eg. Northern Caves). A person who has been down the cave before may be able to suggest some side passages to look at, give route finding or rigging suggestions. There are some experienced cavers who don't like water, heights or tight squeezes. A beginner may get claustrophobic, so be prepared to talk him/her out of the cave.
  • Look at the weather forcast and consider what happens to your choosen cave if it rains or if the snow melts. Some caves can flood to the roof within one hour of it starting to rain.
  • Make sure you have some warm clothing. You will probably get cold and wet in the cave. Pack the equipment you need (ropes, ladders etc.). Ropes should have a knot tied in the end. Remember that you will need a light and helmet. Petzl Zooms are inadequate for caves.
  • Obtain permission to enter the cave if required. To find out if you need permission ask someone who knows the area or look in Northern Caves. The most up to date infomation will be in the CNCC handbook.
  • Tell someone in Lancaster where you are going and what time you expect to be out of the cave(ie. be able to tell that person you're safe either by telephone or in person). If you do not leave the cave in that time they should dial 999 and ask for police and cave rescue. The police will want to know the cave(and route), the number of people and the vehicles used to get there.

    Arriving at the Cave

    So you've survived Bernies' breakfast and have just parked your car at the bottom of a track leading to a cave.
  • Follow the Country Code. Don't leave rubbish. Shut gates. Don't knock walls down etc. Be polite to the farmers since it is their land (and livelyhood) you will be crossing.
  • So you're at the cave, if you are taking beginners then there should be one experienced person near the front and one near the rear of the group. Count the number of people and make sure you don't loose one. Issue warnings about loose boulders, not breaking stalls, drops and not leaving the group.
  • Enjoy the cave

    In the Cave

  • Anyone feeling particularly tired or cold should tell the leader or an experienced person. Do not be embarrised. Either the whole group can then leave or you can be taken out.
  • On arriving at a pitch stay away from the edge. There could be loose rocks that could trip you over and down the pitch. When the rigger or lifeliner says it is safe to come forward do so.
  • Make sure all the other members of your party are safe. It is possible that they will be stuck or scared and all they really need is a friendly voice.
  • At a pitch a lifeliner may not be able to see what is going on and there could be a loud waterfall neaby so a call should be loud enough to be heard. If you should dislodge anything or throw anything down a pitch then shout 'Below' to warn anyone below.
  • Bad air. If you start breathing faster then there is a lot of carbon dioxide in the air. Get out of there.

    Leaving the Cave

  • Make sure all of your group are out. Hopefully you didn't leave any equipment in the cave.
  • Close the gate (or whatever) to the entrance. There is nothing worse than finding a dead sheep in the entrance to a cave.
  • In winter it could well be dark, raining or even snowing. You do remember which way to walk to get back to the car don't you?
